Architecture

One contact model, two CPU solver paths

Controller and robots commands · recovery · logging · physical simulation

Tasks backend

Retains the native reduced decision vector—generalized accelerations plus contact multipliers. Torque is reconstructed by MotionConstr; soft rolling is an ordinary quadratic residual. A repository-local rolling motion constraint updates generalized-force columns without patching Tasks.

TVM backend

Uses explicit point forces and an actuated-only torque variable. Removed graph objects remain in a solver-owned retirement queue until weighted-least-squares caches are refreshed, making repeated remove/re-add and controller destruction deterministic and sanitizer-clean.

Ranger Mini V3 handoff

Visible four-steer model and keyboard teleoperation

The four-steering examples now select the one-argument RollingContactRangerMiniV3 alias. The tracked MuJoCo model is self-contained (no unshipped V2 meshes), uses a 0.494 m wheelbase, 0.364 m track, 0.125 m wheel radius, and has four independently steered and driven wheels.

MuJoCo contact hygiene

The wheels sit inside the visible body envelope. Explicit chassis/wheel collision exclusions prevent artificial self-contact impulses before the first QP solve while retaining wheel/terrain contacts.

RoboticsUtils keyboard

A real terminal starts KeyboardCapture for the keyboard scenario. W/S drive forward/reverse, A/D crab left/right, Q/E yaw left/right, space clears all latched axes, and X stops capture and zeros the controller references. Each axis is latched until its opposite key or space is pressed; the Tasks backend sends chassis and wheel-rate feed-forward to the position/orientation/posture tasks on the next control cycle. The controller synchronizes MuJoCo encoder and FloatingBase measurements into its MBC, then emits measured-state-relative wheel q/alpha references, matching the signals consumed by mc_mujoco. Ctrl-C or closing the MuJoCo window terminates the simulator.

Scope boundary

Known limitations

Deliberate model boundaries

  • Rigid wheels with ideal/contact-mode behavior; no tire deformation, rolling resistance, or complementarity.
  • A four-ray polyhedral friction cone is applied at each of two finite line-contact endpoints.
  • Terrain is a supplied plane or constant-slope ramp; online terrain reconstruction is outside this scope.
  • Provided examples cover differential drive and four independently steering/driving wheels.
  • The planar four-steering specialization selects two independent lateral hard rows to avoid rank duplication.
  • Slip and recovery thresholds require revalidation when wheel materials, terrain, or control period change.
  • Timing is hardware/toolchain-specific evidence, not a portable worst-case execution-time guarantee.
